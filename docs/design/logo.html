Identity

A crest, not an icon

Japanese mon and Bauhaus both reduce an idea to a circle and a few straight lines, and both refuse ornament. The mark sits where they meet: a disc, two cuts, one band. Nothing is drawn that does not carry meaning.

What it says

The disc is the world, and the oldest shape either tradition trusts. The two cuts divide it into left, center and right — the source list, drawn as meridians rather than as a chart.

The magenta band is the annotation ink used across the product: the mark of something read and cited. It sits in the right-hand third and off the center line, so the crest never settles into symmetry.

Construction

One circle on a 100-unit field. Every edge is a vertical at a stated coordinate. There are no curves other than the disc and no angle other than 90°.

One colour

Where the magenta cannot survive — a stamp, an embed, a single-ink print — the band becomes an outline. It inherits currentColor.

How to keep it

Never rotate it

The cuts are meridians. Tilted, they stop meaning anything.

Never recolour the band

Magenta is the annotation ink. It is the one colour the mark owns.

Never centre the band

The asymmetry is the whole design. Centred, it becomes a target.

Never add a container

No rounded square, no ring, no shadow. The disc is already the frame.
